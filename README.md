# Trialign: Beyond Textual Similarity in Clinical Trial Eligibility Matching

### Evaluating and Improving Clinical Meaning Distances in Trial Eligibility Embeddings

**Author:** [Jonathan Ma](https://jonathanma03.github.io), [Jiatong Gao](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jiatonggao/)

TriAlign is a downstream project to [Modern AutoCrit](https://github.com/JonathanMa03/modern-autocrit/tree/main). Modern AutoCrit converts clinical-trial eligibility prose into reviewed, atomic criteria with structured clinical fields and source provenance. TriAlign begins from those outputs and asks a different question: when are two eligibility criteria actually similar in clinical meaning rather than merely similar in wording?

The project will develop an agentic clinical data-science tool for retrieving and comparing eligibility criteria across protocols. It will combine structured checks, dense embeddings, contextual LLM judgments, and human review to identify clinically meaningful equivalence, overlap, restrictiveness, contradiction, and ambiguity. Human-validated comparisons will then be used to improve a dense eligibility-criterion embedding model.

## Motivation

Conventional text embeddings can assign high similarity to criteria that share nearly all their words but define different populations. For example, `age >= 18 years` and `age < 18 years` are textually close but operationally disjoint. Negation, thresholds, units, timing, repetition, exceptions, prior treatment, and Boolean scope can all change clinical meaning without substantially changing the surrounding language.

TriAlign is intended to support practical cross-protocol work such as protocol design, feasibility assessment, competitive-trial review, amendment comparison, evidence synthesis, and retrieval of clinically comparable eligibility rules. It is not intended to make individual patient eligibility decisions.

## Research questions

1. Which clinical phenomena cause conventional embeddings to produce misleading eligibility-criterion rankings?
2. Can a dense encoder fine-tuned on human-validated pairs and clinically difficult hard negatives improve retrieval and relationship prediction over frozen lexical, general, and biomedical baselines?
3. Can an agentic workflow improve labeling quality, uncertainty calibration, and reviewer efficiency while escalating cases that cannot be resolved reliably?

## Data sources

### Primary source: Modern AutoCrit cohort

The primary project data come from the 100-trial oncology and cardiovascular cohort generated by [Modern AutoCrit](https://github.com/JonathanMa03/modern-autocrit/tree/main). The current export is stored at:

```text
data/trial100_v1.xlsx
```

This is the authoritative reviewed extraction, frozen as `data/processed/trial100_v1.parquet`: 3,537 criteria from 100 trials. It preserves structured fields, original evidence, parent statements, trial identifiers and provenance. Corpus review and pair-relationship validation are separate steps. The older unreviewed export remains a local, ignored file.

Historical development pair-review exports remain local under `data/processed/pair_pilot_v1/release_v1/`; their frozen provenance manifest is retained in Git. These are not a held-out clinical benchmark or an approved supervised training set. The [technical document](docs/notes/trialign_technical.tex) records the comparison scopes, remaining actions and family exclusions.

Tracked data are limited to the authoritative extraction workbook, frozen Parquet
and its manifest, the pair-labeling workbook, and the minimal frozen family/split
and contract provenance needed to verify them. Draft JSONL files, construction
packets, pilot snapshots and intermediate audits are ignored and stay local.
Annotation import can use the tracked labeling workbook as its pair registry on
a clean checkout; local construction packets supply additional provenance when
available. Historical audit regeneration requires the corresponding local
evidence files. In `full_data_pilot_v1/`, `workbook.xlsx` is the unchanged frozen
copy of the labeling workbook used to reproduce that pilot, not a second corpus
or another sheet to label. It is ignored along with the rest of that local pilot.

### Alternate source: Chia

[Chia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-020-00620-0) is an expert-annotated corpus of clinical-trial eligibility criteria. It may supplement TriAlign with terminology examples, semantic phenomena, and external evaluation cases. Chia does not replace the Modern AutoCrit cohort and does not provide the project-specific pairwise relationship labels required for TriAlign.

Any Chia-derived records will retain their original provenance, license information, and dataset split. Results from Modern AutoCrit and Chia will be reported separately where their annotation schemes or sampling processes differ.

## Clinical relationship labels

Candidate criterion pairs will be assigned an interpretable relationship rather than collapsed into a single opaque hybrid score:

- equivalent;
- criterion A is more restrictive;
- criterion B is more restrictive;
- partially overlapping or compatible;
- contradictory or effectively disjoint;
- related, but not directly comparable;
- unrelated; or
- ambiguous or supported by insufficient information.

Embedding distance is symmetric, while restrictiveness is directional. TriAlign will therefore treat retrieval and explicit relationship prediction as related but separate tasks.

## Planned methodology

### 1. Review and freeze the source corpus

Review the oncology and cardiovascular criteria against their source evidence, correct extraction errors, preserve ambiguity, and freeze a versioned dataset with provenance.

### 2. Establish frozen baselines and find embedding failures

Compare lexical, general, and biomedical embedding baselines and identify failures involving meaning, negation, thresholds, units, timing, exceptions, and compound logic.

### 3. Agent-assisted labeling with human authority

Use an agent to assemble evidence, select comparison tools, propose relationship labels, reconcile conflicts, quantify uncertainty, and escalate unresolved pairs for authoritative human review.

### 4. Fine-tune a dense representation model

Explore fine-tuning an open dense encoder on reviewed positive pairs, ordinary negatives, hard negatives, and ordered anchors, with a separate component for directional relationships.

### 5. Evaluate statistically and clinically

Evaluate retrieval, relationship prediction, calibration, abstention, reviewer effort, and system components using leakage-resistant trial-family splits and paired uncertainty estimates.

## Intended output

The planned TriAlign application will allow a user to select a criterion, retrieve comparable criteria across protocols, inspect field-level and contextual differences, view an interpretable relationship and uncertainty estimate, and trace every result back to its source evidence. The system will preserve an audit trail of tool results, model and prompt versions, reviewer corrections, and final decisions.

TriAlign is decision support for clinical protocol comparison. Patient-level screening, causal transportability claims, outcome prediction, and a weighted mixture of unrelated scores are outside the primary scope.

## Local working demo

TriAlign runs independently: no Modern AutoCrit checkout, server, environment
file or Python package is required. Its bundled frozen corpus contains the
reviewed extraction outputs. You can also import compatible criterion records
from other sources. A combined extraction/comparison interface remains future work.

From the repository root, create and activate a Python 3.11 or 3.12 environment
and install the application dependencies (first-time setup):

```bash
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python -m pip install -e ".[app,dev]"
```

To start the webapp, activate the environment and run:

```bash
source .venv/bin/activate
python scripts/trialign.py serve --open-browser
```

Keep that terminal open while using the app. Open http://127.0.0.1:8766 if the
browser does not open automatically. Press **Control + C** in the running
terminal to stop the server; closing the browser tab does not stop it. To
restart, run the same start commands. Restart after editing `.env` so the new
configuration is loaded. Saved comparisons and reviews persist across restarts.

If port 8766 is already in use, stop the existing instance or choose another port:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py serve --port 8767 --open-browser
```

Click **Import criteria**, then choose either a combined workbook/files or
separate uploads for Trial A and Trial B. Click **Use uploaded criteria** to
see the input counts and source preview. A two-trial upload selects both trials
automatically and limits the selectors to those uploaded inputs. Comparisons
use exactly those uploaded rows, including subsets of trials already in the
saved corpus. Use **Use saved corpus instead** to return to the bundled data.

XLSX, CSV, Parquet, JSON and JSONL are supported. A workbook may put the trials
on separate sheets, or use a trial-ID column in one sheet. Common criterion-text
headers such as `Criteria`, `Criterion` and `Text` are recognized; optional column
mapping accepts other headers. Files without trial IDs use their sheet or file
names, or their explicitly assigned A/B side. Missing clinical fields remain
unreviewed. Missing inclusion/exclusion roles are marked `unspecified` and need
source review; importing text does not certify extraction or atomicity.

Click **Compare trials** to run. The default processes all retrieved candidate
pairs (up to 5,000), rather than stopping at 16. Criterion counts and candidate-pair
counts differ: retrieval proposes likely counterparts rather than every possible
cross-trial combination. Explicit pair/time limits are reported as limited runs;
Provider settings controls session-configurable budgets: **15 minutes and 200 calls
per comparison by default**, with a time setting of 1–60 minutes and an
**Unlimited calls** option to disable the call cap. There
is no separate 24-call ceiling. The time limit and Stop control still apply.
An exhausted call budget can leave contextual cases unresolved. **Stop comparison** keeps
completed results and starts no further comparisons. An in-flight model request
may take up to 35 seconds to return.

Startup and refresh open a blank comparison form. Choose trials, upload files,
or explicitly select an entry from **Saved comparisons**. The app does not
automatically reopen the last run or upload. Provider settings reset to defaults
on page load; model names, budgets and optional USD token rates can be changed
for the current session. Saved runs retain their own settings independently.

Saved runs, uploaded input snapshots and review histories live under the
Git-ignored repository directory `runs/` and survive restarts. Existing history
from `artifacts/demo/` is copied there once without overwriting newer saved
files or deleting the originals. **Export Excel report** downloads a readable
workbook; JSON remains available through the export endpoint with `?format=json`.
Deleting a completed `run*.json` file removes that comparison from the list
after refresh; deleted history is not imported again from the legacy folder.
Stop an active comparison before deleting its file, since it continues saving
while running. These files do not modify the frozen corpus or annotation release.

New runs and Excel exports record effective provider/model settings, budgets,
retrieval and execution wall time, local worker CPU time, API request latency,
input/output/cached tokens, and per-request usage. Cost estimation uses the USD
input, cached-input and output rates entered in Provider settings. Missing
rates or usage are reported as unavailable/partial, not as zero. Estimates
cover API model tokens only and exclude local compute and other fees.
Historical runs keep their original metadata; unrecorded fields are not invented.

Run filenames use New York time, trial identifiers, execution status, and a short
unique suffix, for example:

```text
run_2026-11-08_15-30-45_EST_NCT01841736_NCT04310007_completed_a1b2c3d4.json
```

Existing filenames are upgraded on server startup while retaining internal run
IDs and reviews. The filename updates when execution status changes. Saved
comparison labels show a compact date/time, the two trial identifiers, status,
and an optional note instead of full protocol titles. For uploaded inputs,
original trial identifiers are used when available.

To annotate a saved run, edit its existing `note` field (for example,
`"Presentation example"`) and optionally `display_status` (for example,
`"Reviewed"`), then refresh the page. Keep the other fields, especially `run_id`
and the execution `status`, unchanged. `display_status` changes the list label
without changing execution state. Manual notes survive later saves and are
included in Excel reports. Filenames and list timestamps use America/New_York;
other recorded metadata timestamps remain UTC.


The working workflow uses planner skills, allowlisted tools, source/quote hooks,
a contextual LLM judge, a source-first blind critic, one bounded revision and explicit escalation.
The critic first makes its own assessment without receiving the judge output,
then reviews the proposal against that fixed assessment. Contextual pairs use
three calls initially, with up to two more for one revision.
The temporary retrieval encoder is TF–IDF; no fine-tuned model or trained
relationship head is being simulated. Disable contextual judge/critic for
local tools-only operation; contextual cases then remain unresolved.

For contextual reasoning, configure `OPENAI_API_KEY` and optionally
`TRIALIGN_JUDGE_MODEL` (default `gpt-4o-mini`). Set `TRIALIGN_CRITIC_MODEL`
to configure a different critic model; otherwise it uses the judge model.
The initial source assessments use separate stateless contexts. Shared model
errors can remain correlated; this is not independent clinical validation. Configuration precedence is the
process environment over an explicitly selected `TRIALIGN_ENV_FILE`, otherwise
the repository `.env`. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` on a fresh checkout and
supply your key. TriAlign does not read Modern AutoCrit configuration. Credentials are read
locally and excluded from run exports. External model calls require connectivity
and incur API usage costs. The UI exposes pair, call and time limits and cancellation.

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py harness
python -m pytest
python scripts/freeze_pair_pilot.py --verify
```

The harness checks constructed routing and constraint cases; passing these and
integration checks does not establish clinical accuracy. See the technical guide
for architecture, provenance, limitations and the remaining evaluation work.

## Family-aware evaluation and manual review

The baselines used the frozen `data/processed/trial100_v1.parquet` corpus (3,537
criteria, 100 trials). Their whole-corpus top-five rankings are exploration
artifacts. For held-out evaluation, fit TF-IDF on training only and rerank each
query against other trials in its own partition; do not filter the old global
top-five lists as a substitute. Pinned dense embeddings can be reused when the
input rendering is identical.

```bash
python scripts/freeze_partitions.py --verify
```

The relevance contract distinguishes direct counterparts (2), related topics
(1), unrelated criteria (0), and uncertain evidence (unscored), independently of
clinical relationship labels. The working workbook at
`data/processed/pair_pilot_v1/trial100_v1_pair_labeling.xlsx` now preserves the
75 pilot decisions and adds 300 training pairs: 210 clinical-question
searches, 60 baseline suggestions and 30 random controls. Start with pair
numbers 76–125; the Guide sheet explains both tasks. A separate sheet contains
60 separate second-pass reviews. The November 9 assistant draft round fills
both tasks and both review sheets, with source quotes, rationale and unresolved
issues. Filter `Review check` for draft disagreements and evidence review first.
These proposals still require final human review; the assistant second pass
does not satisfy the independent human double-review requirement. Validation and test packets are not yet
created. Definitions, reuse caps,
blinding, metrics, duplicate handling and release gates are in the
[technical document](docs/notes/trialign_technical.tex#sec:reviewsampling).

The expanded workbook passes reconciliation of all 75 historical decisions

## Model selection, annotations and training

For the **unvalidated full-data pilot**, the saved snapshot is
`data/processed/pair_pilot_v1/full_data_pilot_v1/`. It includes the unchanged
workbook, partitioned JSONL targets, audit and SHA-256 manifest. There are
370 eligible pairs, all in the frozen training partition, plus five excluded
quarantined pairs. Source issues and draft-review disagreements mask targets:
257 relevance targets and 248 relationship targets remain enabled. The original
workbook remains editable; later changes do not update this snapshot.

Run from the repository with the environment activated:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py train data/processed/pair_pilot_v1/full_data_pilot_v1 docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model --training-config configs/training_pilot.yaml --allow-draft-labels
python scripts/trialign.py evaluate-model docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model data/processed/pair_pilot_v1/full_data_pilot_v1 docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/fit.json --split train --allow-draft-labels
```

Training prints live batch progress, epoch, loss, optimizer-update counts,
elapsed time and an estimated remaining duration. Its working directory is
`docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model.training/`: `training.log` is readable text,
`events.jsonl` retains detailed progress, `progress.json` holds the latest status,
and `checkpoint.pt` holds resumable state. These generated files and model
weights are Git-ignored. Omitting the training output argument defaults to
`docs/logging/<label-release-name>/model`; omitting the fit-report argument
defaults to `docs/logging/<run-name>/fit.json` for `--split train`.

To watch a background training job with automatic exit on completion or pause:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py train-watch docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model
```

Training itself exits after model export. A plain `tail -f` viewer keeps waiting
for future lines after training finishes; Control+C stops that viewer and leaves
the saved model intact. Control+C in `train-watch` also stops only the viewer.
Evaluation is a separate command: it logs input checks, model loading, each batch
of original/reversed comparisons, metric computation and final report saving.
For `fit.json`, its readable log is `fit.evaluation/evaluation.log` beside the
report, with matching `events.jsonl` and `progress.json`. Evaluation reports
`completed` only after the report is atomically saved, then exits; interruption
records `cancelled` and preserves an existing report.

Press **Control+C once** in the training terminal to request a graceful pause,
or run this from another terminal:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py train-pause docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model
```

Wait for the `paused` status before closing the terminal. Training finishes the
current gradient-accumulation group, saves at an optimizer-update boundary and
exits. A pause during validation waits for that epoch's validation to finish.
Resume with the same labels, configuration and output directory:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py train data/processed/pair_pilot_v1/full_data_pilot_v1 docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model --training-config configs/training_pilot.yaml --allow-draft-labels --resume
```

Checkpoints include current and best weights, optimizer, learning-rate schedule,
epoch/batch position, batch order, losses and random states. Resume verifies
label/configuration/code/device and recorded library fingerprints. Changing these
requires a new run. Periodic checkpoints default to every ten optimizer updates,
configurable with `--checkpoint-every-updates`; pauses and epoch boundaries also
save. After an unclean interruption, resume replays work since the last saved
checkpoint. The completed model directory is created only after successful
export. Jobs started with older code cannot gain checkpoints retroactively.

This fine-tunes pinned MPNet and the relationship head for three full epochs
on CPU, with batch size four and four-step gradient accumulation. Parameters
are configurable in `configs/training_pilot.yaml`. Long inputs explicitly allow
recorded truncation at 384 tokens for this pilot. There is no validation set,
early stopping or best-checkpoint selection: the final epoch is saved. The fit
report measures **in-sample agreement with unvalidated draft labels**, not
clinical accuracy or generalization. Draft test evaluation and automatic clinical
runtime registration are disabled. Use a fresh artifact directory for each run.

After training finishes, temporarily make the pilot selectable with this
**backend-only launch option**:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py serve --pilot-model docs/logging/full_data_pilot_v1/model --open-browser
```

Stop the existing server first, or add `--port 8767` for a separate instance.
In Provider settings, select **TriAlign trained encoder — draft pilot (testing)**
for candidate retrieval. Also enable **Use registered TriAlign relationship

The override lasts only for this server process and does not edit `.env` or

The importer verifies source text, IDs, orientation, exact quotes, review fields,
family partitions and independent human second reviews. Draft reviewer names
cannot approve labels. At least 20% of the **released training pairs** and all
held-out judgments need independent human double review. The 60 second-review
slots cover the 300 new pairs; including the historical pilot in a training
release can require additional second reviews. `--allow-partial` explicitly
releases only eligible rows while recording pending rows; it does not relax
these checks. Future validation/test construction packets can be supplied with
repeatable `--packet path/to/packet.jsonl` options. No validation packet is
created automatically, and quarantined pairs remain excluded.

The resulting directory contains `train.jsonl`, `validation.jsonl`, `test.jsonl`
and a checksum manifest. Unknown/source-blocked targets are masked rather than
turned into negatives. Real training requires reviewed training **and validation**
data; the current assistant-prefilled workbook does not meet that requirement.

Install the optional `models` dependencies before training. Edit
[`configs/training.yaml`](configs/training.yaml) for encoder/head learning rates,
encoder freezing, batch size, gradient accumulation, epochs, dropout, head width,
loss weights and margins, swap augmentation, early stopping, seed and device.
For BioLORD, set `base_model: biolord` and `max_seq_length: 128`.
Input truncation fails by default; `allow_recorded` is an explicit experiment
setting, and truncation is recorded. A truncated runtime input cannot produce an
accepted learned-model proposal.

```bash
# Constructed pipeline check only; uses a tiny encoder and no reviewed corpus labels.
python scripts/trialign.py train-smoke docs/logging/learning_smoke_v1

# After an approved release includes validation labels:
python scripts/trialign.py train artifacts/labels/reviewed_v1 docs/logging/reviewed_v1/model --training-config configs/training.yaml
python scripts/trialign.py evaluate-model docs/logging/reviewed_v1/model artifacts/labels/reviewed_v1 docs/logging/reviewed_v1/validation.json
```

The shared encoder learns pair identification from relevance supervision; its
ordered head learns clinical relationships. Artifacts contain the encoder,
head, label map, input format, configuration, input/code hashes, dependency
versions and training history. Existing release/model directories cannot be
overwritten. The smoke check is synthetic and cannot be registered in the app
as a clinical model. Test labels are not opened during training.

Before a final model-only test evaluation, lock its configuration:

```bash
python scripts/trialign.py lock-model-evaluation docs/logging/reviewed_v1/model artifacts/labels/reviewed_v1 docs/logging/reviewed_v1/model_lock.json
python scripts/trialign.py evaluate-model docs/logging/reviewed_v1/model artifacts/labels/reviewed_v1 docs/logging/reviewed_v1/test.json --split test --test-lock docs/logging/reviewed_v1/model_lock.json
```

Model evaluation reports relationship accuracy/F1, swap consistency and
reviewed-pair relevance discrimination separately. These are not full-corpus
retrieval metrics. `evaluate-retrieval --help` describes validation-only pooled
P@5, bounds, nDCG and paired family-group bootstrap intervals; it requires
preselected query IDs and partition-correct ranking lists. Unknown judgments
remain unknown. Full-system test evaluation requires its own locked experiment.

To register a real trained artifact, set `TRIALIGN_MODEL_PATH` in TriAlign's
`.env` or shell environment, then restart the server. Provider settings can use
its encoder, its relationship head, or both. Head outputs are uncalibrated tool
proposals; source checks and the independent judge/critic workflow still apply.
The source-scope harness is available with `python scripts/trialign.py harness`.
Implementation details and limitations are maintained in the
[technical document](docs/notes/trialign_technical.tex).
